## Abstract

Composed "patchwork" systems — many shallow specialist cells wired by simple combiners — are usually evaluated end-to-end, which hides the primitives that make composition work. We study these primitives on a microcosm where the ground truth is exact: every reachable 3×3 tic-tac-toe position is enumerated and solved (180,361 states, digest-frozen), so every claim is falsifiable against the enumeration rather than a sample. Our headline finding is about the space *between* cells. A per-state cell-signature (which of six depth-*k* trees places its top-1 move in the optimal set) predicts whether the composition succeeds on that state with **0.5955 bits of mutual information**, versus **0.1212 bits** for the hand-authored WIN/BLOCK/NON_LOCAL threat taxonomy — and a permutation test (1,000 shuffles, seed 0) puts both far above their nulls (null mean ≤ 0.0006 bits, empirical p = 0.001 for each). The routing surface is a latent segmentation *discovered by measurement*, not the taxonomy in the documentation. It has consequences: an oracle router reaches 0.8698 — a **measured upper bound**, +12.6 points over the static composition's 0.7437 — and a pre-registered confidence-gated router already claims 26.7% of that headroom, scoring **0.7774** (SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC), above the best single shallow tree under either protocol (d6 seed-0 0.7518; 3-seed SEED-MEAN 0.7481, max−min 0.0065). The same measurements locate the wall: pure board-routing KILLs at its pre-registered gate, and a class-blind failure basin (21.9% of states, all six trees wrong) resists every current label. Two further findings frame the arc. Additive voting collapses on defense — a linear voter top-1s the blocking move on 2.2% of blocked positions where an unpruned tree reaches 0.997 — so composed systems need explicit **VETO primitives** (gates, pinchers), not better voters. And an LLM-as-judge distillation target can be *nearly a constant function*: our judge labeled **93 of 96 cells needs-fix on the `blind_split` field and 96 of 96 on the `blind_uniform` field**, so the pre-registered degeneracy guard caps the result at INCONCLUSIVE; constant-baseline checks must precede any judge distillation. Composition *alone* sits at parity with the best single tree — the wins come from gating and routing *between* cells. All experiments ran under pre-registration, with deviations disclosed in a single box (§3).

### Reader's Guide

The paper is a chain of pre-registered experiments (PX0–PX6), each frozen before it ran and each leaving receipts in the repository. How they build the three findings:

- **Finding 1 — additive voting cannot veto; systems need VETO primitives.** PX1 (§4) measures the ceiling ladder and finds additive voters far below it; PX1b (§4) isolates the failure mode as the *defensive override* (BLOCK states); PX2 (§5) builds the veto primitive (pinch cell) and its immune layer, and the blind roster's wiring confirms the prediction in the running system.
- **Finding 2 — the routing surface between cells is real, measurable, and partly capturable.** PX5 (§7) maps the per-state signature space (EXPLORATORY) and finds it predicts composition success ~5× better than the authored taxonomy; the PX5b permutation test (§7) confirms both signal and asymmetry; PX6 (§7, confirmatory) tests capture: pure routing KILLs, confidence-gated routing claims 26.7% of the measured headroom, and the defense split — the exact thing voting could not do — is the routable part. Composition *alone* (§5) sits at parity, honestly reported.
- **Finding 3 — judge degeneracy is real, cheap to detect, and guardable.** PX3 (§6) shows a real LLM-as-judge that is nearly a constant function on both evaluation fields, caught by a pre-registered degeneracy guard that converts a flattering 0.9167 into an honest INCONCLUSIVE.

PX0 (§5) is the ideation round whose three-model convergence produced the immune layer used throughout PX2.

### Terms

Jargon used throughout, defined once here:

- **top1-in-optimal** — the fraction of test states where a model's argmax move is *in* the state's optimal-move set (labels are optimal-move *sets* from an exact solver, so quality is a membership test, not a preference judgment). Set-recall travels alongside in every booked results file; this paper quotes top1 only.
- **signature** — the 6-bit pattern of which trees d1–d6 place their top-1 in the optimal set on a given state. 64 possible values; only 15 occur.
- **pinch cell** — a hand-coded two-rule veto specialist: play center if empty, else block an opponent two-in-line threat; *abstain* otherwise. It can override the vote rather than join it.
- **format gate** — a structural check that a cell emitted 9 finite floats; fails loud on anything else.
- **immune layer** — the gardener-loop controls from PX0: a sanity cell with a 5/5 gate before any numbers are collected, 5% silently shadowed no-op moves (logged sidecar-only), a fixed 1,000-state drift re-check every 10 moves, and a BLOCK-split report on every eval.
- **mutual information (MI)** — $I(X;Y) = \sum_{x,y} p(x,y)\log_2 \frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}$, in bits: how many bits knowing $X$ tells you about $Y$. First used in §7, with its permutation null.
- **SEED-MEAN / SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC** — reporting protocols. SEED-MEAN: mean over tree/walk seeds, spread quoted as max−min. SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC: one run of a pipeline with no stochastic component (or a fixed seed), quoted as the single value it is. Every headline score in this paper is labeled.

## 1 Introduction

Modern composed systems — sparse mixture-of-experts, ensembles, routers over model zoos, LLM pipelines stitched from tool calls — are usually argued about in the single-model vocabulary: capacity, loss, benchmark score. We claim this vocabulary hides three primitives that composed systems actually need, and we rank them by what our measurements say matters most:

1. **A measured routing surface BETWEEN cells** — the per-state pattern of which cells succeed — which is empirically a different, and more useful, object than any taxonomy the system's authors write down. This is the paper's headline: on our microcosm the signature→success MI (0.5955 bits, permutation-confirmed against a null of ≤ 0.0006 bits) is ~5× the signature→taxonomy MI (0.1212 bits, also permutation-confirmed), an oracle router's **measured upper bound** is 0.8698 against the composition's 0.7437, and a pre-registered confidence-gated router already banks 26.7% of that headroom (0.7774, SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC) — beating the best single shallow tree under either protocol, where composition *alone* only reaches parity.
2. **Dedicated specialist cells** whose competence is *local* to identifiable regions of the input space, not uniform — and **VETO primitives** (gates, pinchers) that can override or abstain, because some failure modes of voting are not fixable by more or better voters: the defensive override, where a linear voter top-1s the blocking move on 2.2% of blocked positions and even depth-3 reaches only 0.24, while an unpruned tree reaches 0.997.
3. **Evaluator defenses** — because a distillation target can be nearly a constant function (our judge: 93/96 needs-fix on `blind_split`, 96/96 on `blind_uniform`) and a pre-registered degeneracy guard is what converts a publishable-looking WIN into an honest INCONCLUSIVE.

Studying these primitives at LLM scale is expensive and noisy: labels are contested, ceilings are unknown, and every result is confounded. We therefore build the argument on a microcosm where the ground truth is exact. The terrain is 3×3 tic-tac-toe: all 180,361 reachable our-turn positions, each labeled with the *set* of optimal moves from a minimax solver. Nothing is sampled; nothing is estimated from a test set of human labels. Every performance number below is a top1-in-optimal rate over a frozen 36,073-state test split, and every claim can be re-derived from the committed enumeration (§3). The microcosm is small — that is the point. Small means exact, and exact means falsifiable: ceilings are measured quantities rather than assumptions, degeneracy is detectable rather than suspected, and failure basins can be attributed to the structure of the function rather than to annotation noise.

The paper is organized as that chain of experiments (the Reader's Guide above maps them). §3 describes the terrain, the discipline, and a single disclosure box holding every pre-registration deviation. §4 shows additive voting collapsing on the defensive override and derives the veto-primitive design consequence. §5 builds the patchwork harness with its immune layer, reports the composition/d6 comparison under both protocols with the mixed-protocol caveat explicit, separates the blind-roster defense finding from the live-arm adjudication bar, and discloses the live arm as pending-with-bar. §6 shows the near-constant judge and the guard. §7 measures the routing surface, confirms it against a permutation null, and reports the pre-registered router test: pure routing KILLs, gating+fallback claims 26.7% of the measured headroom, and the defense split is the routable part. §8 condenses design principles; §9 lists limitations; §10 lists next steps.

What we do *not* claim: no scaling claims of any kind; no claim that the current patchwork's static combiner beats a deep tree (it does not — it sits at parity with the best *shallow* tree, and §5 says so plainly under both protocols); no claim that the remaining router headroom is capturable — 26.7% is claimed by one deterministic run of one gated router, the rest is a measured upper bound, not yet earned.

## 2 Related work

**Mixture-of-experts and routing.** Sparse MoE conditions computation on learned routers over expert subnetworks (Shazeer et al., 2017); scaled variants established the modern switching family (Fedus, Zoph & Shazeer, 2022) [CITE-MISSING: expert-choice routing paper]. Classic hierarchical mixtures of experts treated gating as conditional probability mixture (Jacobs, Jordan, Nowlan & Hinton, 1991). This literature optimizes routers *jointly* with experts by gradient flow. Our microcosm studies the opposite regime: cells fixed and cheap, combiners trivial (majority vote, tie to lowest index), and the routing surface measured *post hoc* as an empirical object — closer to ensemble/abstention analysis [CITE-MISSING: deep ensembles; Chow's rejection rule] than to MoE training. Cascade classifiers are the nearest relative of our pinch cell: a cheap specialist that fires on a decisive sub-population and abstains otherwise [CITE-MISSING: Viola–Jones cascade classifier, CVPR 2001].

**LLM-as-judge.** Using a strong model to grade weaker outputs is now standard practice (Zheng et al., 2023), with known biases (position, verbosity, self-preference) documented [CITE-MISSING: judge-bias literature]. PX3 (§6) contributes a blunt addition to that list: a judge can be *approximately constant* on a whole evaluation field — agreeing with a distilled student at 0.9167 (= 88/96) while carrying almost no per-cell structural signal. The check is one line; we argue it should be mandatory.

**Solved games as instrumentation.** Small solved games have long served as clean testbeds [CITE-MISSING: tic-tac-toe game-theoretic value, standard reference; endgame databases in checkers, Schaeffer et al., Science 2007; self-play line, AlphaZero, Science 2018]. We use the enumeration not to study play strength but as a *labeling instrument*: labels are optimal-move sets, so model quality is a membership test, not a preference judgment.

**Pre-registration culture.** Commit-first pre-registration, frozen branch tables, and deviation disclosure come from clinical trials and the replication movement [CITE-MISSING: CONSORT statement; Nosek et al. on preregistration, PNAS 2018; registered reports], and exist in ML mainly as checklists [CITE-MISSING: NeurIPS reproducibility checklist]. Here the discipline is load-bearing method: the PX3 degeneracy guard (§6) and the PX2 blind-control ruling (§5) both bite *because* they were frozen before the data existed. Citation policy: bracketed placeholders are claims the review panel should supply or verify; we have deliberately fabricated no citations.

## 3 An exact ground truth

> **Disclosure box — all pre-registration deviations, in one place.** (1) *PX1b post-hoc commit*: PX1b's pre-registration (classes, branch table) was frozen in the runner docstring *before* the run but committed *after*, alongside results — violating commit-first. What this does and does not threaten is detailed in the flagged box at §4's start. (2) *Blind gardener budget violation*: the first blind-gardener run (seed 0) stopped at 10 moves, under the pre-registered 50-move budget; its 0.855 endpoint was briefly celebrated, then re-adjudicated with budget-matched seeded runs (§5). (3) *Fire-5 crash/relaunch*: the live gardener's fifth fire was aborted fail-loud by a flag-gate at move 21 and relaunched *armed* as fire 5b (18:25 AKDT) — in flight at revision time; §5 keeps the live arm pending-with-bar. Each deviation also carries a dated amendment in the ledger (`RESULTS.md`); none was resolved by changing a frozen gate.

**Terrain.** The donor repository (SuperInstance/pie-minimax, read-only clone at `main`) contains an exact minimax solver. We enumerate all 180,361 reachable our-turn positions and store, per state, the optimal-move set. Provenance is a FNV-1a-64 digest over the (state + optimal-set) stream: `0x75f652bc1d8464b8`. Representation for all learners is 9 floats per board (row-major), identical to the donor's own linear expert — no feature engineering advantage is traded in either direction.

**Metric.** Everywhere in this paper, model quality is

$$\mathrm{top1}(f) \;=\; \Pr_{s \in \text{test}}\big[\,\arg\max_c f(s)_c \in \mathcal{O}(s)\,\big],$$

where $\mathcal{O}(s)$ is the optimal-move set. Set-recall (fraction of $\mathcal{O}(s)$ recovered by the model's ranked moves) is reported alongside top1 in every booked results file, per the house rule that the pair always travels together. Chance-level top1 depends on $|\mathcal{O}(s)|$; all headline comparisons in this paper are model-vs-model on the same split, never model-vs-chance.

**Split and reporting protocols.** PX1's seed-0 80/20 split: 144,288 train / 36,073 test. Three tree seeds (0/1/2) are run for variance; the split frozen at seed 0 is the one all later experiments (PX2, PX5, PX6) inherit. Trees are scikit-learn `DecisionTreeRegressor`, `min_samples_leaf=5`, `random_state=seed`, trained on the train split only. Unless a score is labeled SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC, PX1-family numbers are SEED-MEANs over seeds 0/1/2 with spread quoted as max−min. Two conventions prevent a recurring confusion: (i) the *tree* seeds are 0/1/2, while the *blind gardener* walk seeds are 1/2/3 — because seed 0 was consumed by the under-budget 10-move pilot disclosed above (§5); (ii) deterministic pipelines (the majority-vote composition, the format gate, the pinch cell, the PX3 student, the PX6 routers) produce a single value by construction — they are labeled SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC and never differenced against a SEED-MEAN as if the sign of the difference were a finding.

**Why exactness matters.** Four reasons, each earning its keep later. (i) *Ceilings are measurements*: the deep-tree ceiling in §4 is a number, not a hope. (ii) *Controls are exact*: PX3's noise and oracle controls reproduce bit-exactly, so any drift found is the instrument's, not the terrain's. (iii) *Degeneracy is detectable*: a test field whose labels are single-class is provably useless for agreement claims, and we can assert that before believing a 0.9167. (iv) *Failure basins are attributable*: when 21.9% of the test split defeats every tree (§7), that is a fact about the function's structure, not about annotators.

**Pre-registration protocol.** Every PX experiment freezes its question, branch table, and controls before data collection, and carries a status label: PX1 CONFIRMATORY (docstring-frozen, commit-first); PX1b CONFIRMATORY with a disclosed post-hoc commit (§4 box); PX2 CONFIRMATORY (file-frozen at `proposals/runs/PX2-patchwork-3x3.md`), live arm PENDING; PX3 CONFIRMATORY (commit-first chain `0b5b88d` → `ee99135` → `62f256a` → `f8c82e3`, pre-reg → instrument → receipts → result; ruling INCONCLUSIVE by frozen guard); PX5 EXPLORATORY (`EXPLORATORY_MAP`, frozen commit-first at `ecc8288` — analyses fixed before scoring, but no held-out confirmatory test); PX6 CONFIRMATORY (commit-first, gates frozen, KILL declared a real outcome). House rules: outcomes may not switch branches post hoc; every deviation gets a dated amendment in the ledger; results book only to the pre-named results files.

## 4 The collapse of additive voters

> **PX1b disclosure box — what was frozen, what was not, and what follows.** PX1b's runner docstring, written *before* the run, froze: the three class definitions (below, with provenance digest `0xa0b08dfac4d13c57`) and the branch table P1–P4 — *including* the ordering prediction P1 (linear degrades monotonically WIN > BLOCK > NON_LOCAL), which was therefore pre-stated and **falsified** by the data, with the P4 kill branch honored. What was *not* frozen pre-run: the commit ordering. The docstring was committed post-run, alongside results (`2d4009b`, 16:19 AKDT) — the pre-run freeze is evidenced by the docstring content and a later bit-exact reproduction check (`ce517bf`), not by a commit timestamp, and a reader cannot verify the ordering from git history alone. We flag this as the boundary case that motivated the strict commit-first discipline used thereafter. One consequence reaches beyond §4: the BLOCK-split diagnostic reported in §5 and §7 was **not** part of the PX2 pre-registration document — it enters the ledger in the same commit as PX1b's results, i.e., after PX1b's outcome was known — and is disclosed here as a post-hoc diagnostic adopted *because of* PX1b.

**PX1 — the ceiling ladder** (CONFIRMATORY, docstring-frozen). On the frozen split, an unpruned tree reaches **0.9987 ± 0.0002** top1-in-optimal (SEED-MEAN over seeds 0/1/2; per-seed 0.9985 / 0.9989 / 0.9985): the ceiling of this representation is, for practical purposes, the exact function. The donor's own linear expert — an additive voter over cell values — retrained on the same split reaches **0.1978 ± 0.005** (SEED-MEAN), i.e. 19.8% of ceiling. The depth ladder interpolates (SEED-MEANs): depth-3 **0.4811**, depth-4 **0.6024**, depth-6 **0.7481** (per-seed d6: 0.7518 / 0.7453 / 0.7471; max−min 0.0065). Two structural facts follow. First, the task is nearly learnable from 9 floats by a piecewise-constant function, so every gap below is a *representation/structure* gap, not a data gap. Second, most of the achievable win is cheap (d3 alone is 2.4× linear), which is what makes the microcosm a fair testbed for *shallow* cells.

**PX1's surprise, carried forward.** The pre-registered hypothesis was that additive voting fails where threats must be *counted* (composed states with multiple immediate wins). The data flipped the sign: linear voters do *better* on composed states (≥2 immediate wins: **0.2640**) than on simple ones (0–1 immediate wins: **0.1895**). Where several wins exist, each win lights up its own local terms and any of them tops the argmax — additive voting is *fine* on offense. Whatever kills the linear voter is not threat-counting.

**PX1b — isolating the failure mode** (CONFIRMATORY with disclosed post-hoc commit; see box above). PX1b partitions the enumeration by threat locality: **WIN** — an immediate win exists and all optimal moves are wins (109,392 states, 60.6%); **BLOCK** — no win exists, the opponent holds a two-in-line threat, and all optimal moves are blocking cells (29,632 states, 16.4%); **NON_LOCAL** — everything else (41,337 states, 22.9%). (The three shares sum to 99.9%, not 100%, because of rounding.) Per-class top1 on the test split (3 seeds):

| class | linear | d3 | d6 | deep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WIN (60.6%) | 0.25 | 0.55 | 0.84 | 0.9998 |
| BLOCK (16.4%) | **0.022 ± 0.003** | 0.24 | 0.57 | 0.997 |
| NON_LOCAL (22.9%) | 0.17 | 0.49 | 0.64 | 0.996 |

The ordered degradation the pre-registration predicted (WIN > BLOCK > NON_LOCAL) did not hold; the honest ordering is WIN > NON_LOCAL > BLOCK. Additive voting does not die on non-local play — it dies on **defense**. When the opponent holds a threat, the correct move is locally unremarkable, while the voter's own-line terms all light up in favor of continuing to build. A linear voter tops-1 the blocking cell on **2.2%** of BLOCK states; even a depth-3 tree only reaches 0.24 there. The deep tree does not collapse (0.997): the defensive override is *representable* in 9 floats — it is just not *additive* and not *shallow*.

**Design consequence.** Voters score; they cannot veto. When the dominant failure mode is "the collective keeps saying yes to its own plan," the fix is not more voters or better-calibrated voters — it is a component with the *right and the ability to override*: a gate that vetoes illegal/degenerate outputs, a pincher that fires a known answer on a decisive sub-population and abstains otherwise, an immune layer that can refuse to act. PX2 was designed around this consequence, including a sharpened sub-test: every patchwork evaluation must report the BLOCK split specifically, because voting-only stacks bleed exactly there (a post-PX1b addition — see the disclosure box).

## 5 Patchwork composition and the immune layer

**Cells.** PX2's launch roster (CONFIRMATORY, file-frozen): depth-$k$ trees ($k \le 6$) trained on the train split only; a **PinchCell** (play center if empty, else block an opponent two-in-line; abstain otherwise — a hand-coded veto specialist built directly from §4); a **FormatGate** (structural check that a cell emitted 9 finite floats); a **MajorityVote** combiner (argmax of votes, ties to lowest index). Local small models and an embedding-based nearest-neighbor gate were wired but flag-gated off; nothing below depends on them.

**Smoke gate.** Before any gardener ran, the harness had to show that plain composition beats its own inputs: majority-vote(d3, d4) with the format gate scored **0.595** vs d3-alone **0.435** on the frozen 200-state quick-eval (+0.160; both SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC). Composition passes its first honesty bar: wiring shallow cells is not a no-op.

**Full-roster composition — an honest miss, under both protocols.** The complete static roster (trees d1–d6 plus pinch, MajorityVote) scores **0.7437** top1 on the full 36,073-state test split (**SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC** — deterministic cells, deterministic tie-break, one run at tree seed 0; measured inside PX5's instrument). The pre-registered cheap-baseline control says the patchwork must beat a depth-6 tree fit on identical train states. Under the two available protocols, d6 is **0.7518** at seed 0 (the composition's own tree seed — the like-for-like number) and **0.7481** as a 3-seed SEED-MEAN (seeds 0/1/2, per-seed 0.7518 / 0.7453 / 0.7471, max−min 0.0065). The composition sits 0.0081 below same-seed d6 and 0.0044 below the d6 mean — **at parity within seed noise, under a mixed-protocol caveat we now make explicit rather than bury in a parenthetical**: one arm is a deterministic combiner (no seed distribution exists for it by construction), the other is a mean over seeds. We do not subtract across protocols and call the sign of the difference a finding. We report the miss as the finding it is: majority vote over a broad roster does not automatically beat the best member, and the justification for the patchwork program cannot rest on the static combiner. Where the justification *does* rest is measured in §7.

**Headroom, defined once.** Define a state as **any-cell-correct** if at least one roster cell — any of the six trees, or the pinch cell on a fired-and-correct result — places its top-1 in the optimal set. On the same split this **measured upper bound** is **0.8698**: +12.6 points above the composition's 0.7437. This is an existence proof that routing between these cells could pay, not a claim that a trained router will capture it (§7 measures how much *was* captured: 26.7%, by gating, not by pure routing).

**The blind-control correction.** PX2's controls include a *blind gardener*: the same move-receipt protocol as the live agent, but moves drawn by seeded RNG from legal wiring moves. Its purpose is to price the harness's random-walk baseline so a live agent must beat it *and* pass a receipt audit. The first blind run (seed 0, 10 moves) ended at **0.855** — suspiciously high, and briefly celebrated; this was the budget violation disclosed in §3. The correction is the part that matters: 10 moves is not the pre-registered budget, and the blind walk has **no ratchet** — the endpoint is where the walk stopped, not the best state seen along the way. Budget-matched serial 50-move runs (seeds 1/2/3 — the pilot consumed seed 0) scored **0.745 / 0.745 / 0.755** — mean **0.748**, spread **max−min = 0.010** (SD ≈ 0.0058 if SD is preferred; we state both so no reader has to guess). Two lessons generalize. First, single-endpoint anecdotes from stochastic search are noise; match the budget and run seeds before believing any delta. Second, the blind walks' discovered wiring repeatedly paired a pinch cell with depth-6 trees.

**The blind roster owns the defense split.** Separately from anything live: the blind roster's discovered pinch+d6 wiring, and the full-split PX5 measurement behind it, confirm §4's prediction in the running system — the pinch cell fires on 100% of BLOCK states and is correct on **97.6%** of them (`results/px5_disagreement/px5_map.json`), against linear's 0.022 on the same class. The veto specialist owns the defense split that voting cannot. This is a *blind-roster and static-measurement* finding; it is deliberately stated in its own paragraph so it is not conflated with the live arm's adjudication below.

**The live arm: pending, with its bar stated precisely.** The live arm — a GLM-5.3 gardener wiring cells via a coding-plan endpoint — remains in flight, and we cite no numbers from it. Its pre-registered adjudication bar (booked in the PX2 ledger entry) is: **(a)** beat the blind endpoint band — the budget-matched 0.748 SEED-MEAN over seeds 1/2/3, max−min 0.010 — *and* **(b)** pass the receipt-direction audit: a fraction **> 0.5** of gardener moves whose predicted effect direction matches the measured direction. We flag a weakness the panel named: for a binary direction, > 0.5 is chance-level, so this bar certifies only *strictly better than a coin flip* — the pre-registered threshold is what it is, and we will not strengthen it post hoc; at adjudication we will report the raw direction-match rate alongside the pass/fail, and future pre-registrations should state margins strictly above chance. The fire history is a case study in fail-loud infrastructure: Fire 1 — thinking mode consumed the 400-token output budget, empty content, abstain cascade (aborted); Fire 2 — `thinking: disabled` honored on easy moves but *adaptive* thinking ignores the flag on hard wiring moves (17k-character reasoning trace, `finish_reason=length` at 4096); Fire 3 — killed for the 16,384-token cap fix; Fire 4 — 120-second read timeout, fixed with 240 s, transport failures converted to *receipted abstains*, and one clean same-prompt retry with no outcome information visible to the agent; Fire 5 — aborted fail-loud by a flag-gate at move 21; **Fire 5b — relaunched armed (18:25 AKDT), still walking at revision time.** Every abort left a receipt; no number from the live arm enters this paper. **[CITE-MISSING: final adjudication — the live GLM gardener's endpoint score and receipt-direction audit, to be booked in `results/px2_patchwork/` and cited only from there; it will enter this paper as a numbered addendum to §5 carrying its own timestamp.]** Operational footnote from the ledger: same-second parallel launches collided on timestamped receipt filenames (three seeds, one file); serial launches are now the rule.

**The immune layer.** PX0 — an ideation round with three independent models — converged on the same diagnosis: the dominant risk in a gardener loop is not bad cells but *gardener epistemics* (format-pass/semantically-broken outputs, silent drift, post-hoc rationalization). Their proposed controls composed into PX2's immune layer, which ran on every session: (i) a cataloged sanity cell must pass a 5/5 gate against known ground-truth positions before any numbers are collected; (ii) 5% of gardener moves are silently replaced by no-ops, logged sidecar-only — if the gardener's receipt quality is indistinguishable between accepted and rejected moves, it is rationalizing, and all WIN/PARTIAL rulings are void; (iii) a fixed 1,000-state drift check re-scores every 10 moves; (iv) the BLOCK split is reported on every eval. None of these are exotic; all of them exist because the failure mode is the *evaluator*, not the model.

## 6 When the judge is a constant

**Setup** (CONFIRMATORY, commit-first, guard frozen in the pre-reg). PX3 tests whether a real LLM-as-judge can be *distilled*: a local student replacing the judge's 288 API calls per field with one forward pass. The judge is a hosted model (jev-1.13.0 via a typesafe `/v1/systemone` endpoint) answering correct/needs-fix per cell of an 8×12 synthetic field. Two blind fields (`blind_split`, `blind_uniform`), 96 judged cells each, rebuilt at three budgets: 576 calls total, 0 errors, receipts digest `0x29ebeffdce727a44`. The student embeds state strings with a local embedding model (768-d) and fits an L2-regularized logistic head in numpy — fully deterministic.

**The reproduction control earns its keep.** The house rule is that reproducing a stored result is a control, not a formality. Noise and oracle control MAEs reproduce bit-exactly; the uniform-field judge deltas reproduce exactly (+0.0000, ×3); but the split-field judge deltas drift in magnitude (direction holds), and a stored free-statistic correlation (−0.184) is irreproducible (fresh: −0.156) on an offline-*deterministic* quantity — that stored line predates the final fields module. Verdict: harness identical; the judge itself drifted across runs; the stored analysis was stale. Within-run, duplicate-triple label consistency is 1.0, so the drift is cross-run model-side, not within-run noise.

**Evals, and the guard that matters — raw counts throughout.** Within-field agreement on `blind_split` is 0.9583 (**92/96**; per-class F1 0.50 / 0.98 — with only 3 correct-class states, the F1 pair is exactly as lopsided as it should be); within-UNIFORM is 1.0 (trivial). The real claim is cross-field: trained on all `blind_split` cells, tested on all `blind_uniform` cells, the student agrees at **0.9167 = 88/96**. The pre-registered WIN zone was agreement ≥ 0.85, i.e. ≥ 82/96 — the observed 88/96 sits inside it, with 6 cells of margin. A bootstrap CI on that agreement is **[0.854, 0.969]** (1,000 resamples, seed 0) — but because the guard (below) fires, this is a **CI on a degenerate quantity, shown only to demonstrate the guard's necessity**: against a single-class test field, "agreement" is the student's majority-class rate, and the interval carries no inferential content about distillation. The pre-registration also froze a **degeneracy guard**: if the test field's labels are single-class, agreement cannot distinguish a student from a constant, and the ruling is capped at INCONCLUSIVE. The guard fires: `blind_uniform` is **96/96 needs-fix**. The flattering 88/96 is capped to **INCONCLUSIVE**, as pre-committed.

**Mechanism discovery.** Why does a "judge ≈ noise" stored result coexist with 88/96 agreement? Because the judge is *approximately a constant function on both blind fields*: **93/96** cells labeled needs-fix on `blind_split`, **96/96** on `blind_uniform`. A nearly-constant label function agrees with any student that predicts the majority label almost everywhere — and carries no structural signal to distill. The stored "judge ≈ noise" was never about judgment quality in the human sense; it is what a threshold-biased near-constant looks like in a noise-delta harness. The three `correct` outliers on the split field are the only structure present, and the student found two of them.

**Consequences — and the scope of the evidence.** (i) Any LLM-as-judge distillation — arguably any judge *use* — must run a constant-baseline check before any agreement number is interpreted. The check is one line: label distribution of the field, and student-vs-majority-label agreement. (ii) Judges need calibration audits per prompt-format, not per benchmark: this judge's threshold/bias on scene-format prompts makes it a selector that selects nothing. (iii) The pre-registered guard did the exact job it was frozen for: it converted a publishable-looking WIN into an honest INCONCLUSIVE *before* anyone had to decide under incentive load. (iv) Scope, stated plainly: the constancy result is one judge, one prompt format, two 96-cell *synthetic* fields — two data points, not a survey. It establishes that the failure mode exists and is cheap to detect, not that it is common. The replication plan is concrete: re-run the same constant-baseline check and distillation pipeline on real benchmark judges (§9, §10) before any generality is claimed.

## 7 The signature map

**PX5 — the census** (EXPLORATORY_MAP, frozen commit-first at `ecc8288`; analyses fixed before scoring, no confirmatory branch — the routing-surface *claim* below is exploratory, and §7's PX6 is its first confirmatory probe of *routability*, not of orthogonality). PX5 asks the question the single-model view cannot even pose: *what does the space between cells look like?* The instrument: for every test state, record the 6-bit **signature** of which trees d1–d6 place their top-1 in the optimal set, crossed with the pinch cell's axis (fired-correct / fired-wrong / abstain), the PX1b threat class, and whether the full composition (§5) is correct. Terrain digest asserted; full 36,073-state test split; no training involved — this is pure measurement of an existing system.

Of 64 possible tree-signatures, only **15** occur across 36,073 states. The cells' success patterns are heavily structured: the six trees do not behave as interchangeable noisy voters; they carve the state space into a few large, repeatable regimes.

**The asymmetry, now with a null.** Mutual information (defined in the Terms box), in bits:

- $I(\text{signature} \to \text{composition-correct}) = \mathbf{0.5955}$ (exact: 0.595498)
- $I(\text{signature} \to \text{threat class}) = \mathbf{0.1212}$ (exact: 0.121228)

Because MI estimators are biased upward with many bins, and because a point estimate with no uncertainty made this claim unfalsifiable (the stats reviewer's point, conceded), we computed the permutation null (`experiments/px5_permtest.py`, numpy only, seed 0). PX5's runner had not persisted per-state artifacts, so the per-state (signature, composition-correct, class) vectors were **regenerated through the frozen instrument machinery** (same seeds, terrain digest asserted) and **cross-checked against the booked `px5_map.json`** — test size, 15-signature census, composition 0.7437, both MIs to four decimals, and the 9,470/7,895 unanimous partitions all reproduce, fail-loud on any mismatch — before anything was booked. The joinable per-state artifact is now persisted (`results/px5_disagreement/per_state.npz`). Results, effective cardinality first: the MI is computed over the **15 observed** signature values × 2 classes (30 joint cells) for the success MI, and 15 × 3 (45 cells) for the taxonomy MI — not 64×2.

- **T1, signature → composition-correct:** observed **0.5955** bits; permutation null (1,000 shuffles of the label vector, which preserves the label marginal) **mean 0.0003, sd 0.0001, max 0.0007**; empirical p = **0.001** (zero of 1,000 shuffles reached the observed value; p = (1+0)/(1+1000)).
- **T2, signature → threat class:** observed **0.1212** bits; null **mean 0.0006, sd 0.0001, max 0.0011**; empirical p = **0.001**.
- **Miller-Madow bias note:** the plug-in MI's upward bias at these cardinalities is ≈ 0.00028 bits (T1, 15×2) and ≈ 0.00056 bits (T2, 15×3) at N = 36,073 — the same order as the null means and orders of magnitude below both observations; no correction changes any conclusion.

Both MIs are decisively above their nulls, so the finding is *not* "the taxonomy MI is zero"; it is that the routing surface carries ~5× more information about composition success than about the authored taxonomy — and both associations are real. Knowing *which cells succeed here* strongly predicts whether the composition gets the state — and barely predicts the hand-authored WIN/BLOCK/NON_LOCAL taxonomy. The routing surface is nearly **orthogonal to the author taxonomy**: it is a latent segmentation of the state space that was *discovered by the instrument*, not designed by the authors. The map that matters for routing is not the taxonomy in the documentation; it is the empirical correlation structure of cell success, and it is cheap to measure.

**Unanimous partitions — and the mixed middle.** 9,470 states (26.3%) are all-6-correct — reflex-compile candidates. 7,895 states (**21.9%**) are all-6-wrong (every tree individually wrong). The remaining **18,708 states (51.9%) have mixed signatures** — partial overlap is the norm, and the unanimous partitions are the extremes, not the typical state. The all-6-wrong basin is class-blind, spread across the authored classes (WIN 2,812 / BLOCK 2,392 / NON_LOCAL 2,691). No current class vocabulary explains the basin; whatever it is, the cells share a systematic bias that the threat taxonomy does not name. This is a concrete, falsifiable target: any new cell or training change should be adjudicated partly on whether it dents the 21.9%.

**Reconciling the basin with per-tree BLOCK scores.** The BLOCK class has 5,924 test states, of which 2,392 (40.4%) are all-6-wrong — while §4 reports d6 alone at 0.57 on BLOCK (wrong on 42.25%, from the booked per-cell matrix: 0.5775 correct). These reconcile exactly: **all-6-wrong is a strictly stronger condition than d6-wrong** (if every tree is wrong then d6 is wrong, so the all-6-wrong rate must sit at or below the d6-wrong rate — 40.4% ≤ 42.25%, as observed). Note also that all-6-trees-wrong does not mean the *collective* fails: wrong votes scatter, and the composition — whose vote can still land on an optimal move when the trees disagree about *how* to be wrong — rescues 10.75% of the basin (booked `comp_top1` on the all-6-wrong partition).

**Router headroom — and how much was captured.** The any-cell-correct **measured upper bound** (definition as fixed in §5) is **0.8698** vs the composition's 0.7437 — **+12.6 points**. PX6 (below) is the pre-registered first attempt to claim it.

**Pinch pre-emption.** The pinch cell fires on 72.5% of states (66.35% correct, 6.15% wrong) and abstains on 27.5%. A hand-coded two-rule veto specialist is *correct on the vast majority of the space where it dares speak* — and its 6.15% wrong-fire rate is exactly the kind of number a routing layer must learn to gate, not to ignore.

**PX6 — the router test** (CONFIRMATORY, frozen commit-first; both arms and the gate frozen before scoring; KILL declared a real outcome; no threshold tuning, no arm selection after seeing numbers). Arm (a): a pure depth-6 decision-tree router trained on board states to select the best cell. Pre-registered gate: PARTIAL if routed top1 ≥ 0.759 (≥ 12.5% of the +12.6-point headroom), KILL below. **Arm (a) scores 0.7545 — KILL**, claiming 8.6% of headroom: the routing signal is not board-predictable at this depth. Arm (b): the same router with a confidence gate — when the router's own probability tops out below 0.5, fall back to the static majority-vote composition. **Arm (b) scores 0.7774** (SINGLE-DETERMINISTIC: one run, router `random_state=0`, seed-0 split; no router-seed distribution exists yet — a replication across seeds is queued, §10), which is **above d6 under both protocols** (seed-0 0.7518, 3-seed mean 0.7481) and claims **26.7% of the measured headroom**. The per-class content is the real result (arm a: WIN 0.819, BLOCK 0.873, NON_LOCAL 0.497; arm b: WIN 0.836, BLOCK 0.916, NON_LOCAL 0.522): **the defensive override — the exact thing PX1b said voting cannot do — is board-routable; positional play is not, at this depth.** The wall is NON_LOCAL, matching PX5's class-blind basin. The router is not degenerate (no cell receives > 95% of predictions). Honest arc, then: composition alone at parity; composition **plus gating plus routing** beats the best single tree; pure routing alone does not work. A related probe tempers one §10 idea from v0: the pinch cell already fires on 88.9% of the 37,838 train all-correct states, so the reflex-compile lane would add little on this terrain — the existing reflex owns that partition.

## 8 Design principles

1. **Measure the ceiling before interpreting any score.** Linear 0.1978 reads as "bad model" until the deep tree shows 0.9987 — then it reads as "additive representation, 19.8% of ceiling." Every fraction-of-ceiling claim in a composed system should be backed by a measured ceiling (§4).
2. **Additive voters cannot veto; give the system explicit VETO primitives.** The defensive override (BLOCK: linear 0.022, d3 0.24, deep 0.997) is invisible to aggregate scores and fatal in play. Gates and pinchers that can override or abstain are a different *kind* of component, not a better voter (§4) — and the routing result says the defense split is exactly the routable part (§7).
3. **Composition must beat its parts — including the best single cheap member — and say so when it doesn't.** d3+d4+gate beats d3 (0.595 vs 0.435, single values); the full static roster sits at parity with d6 (0.7437 vs 0.7518 seed-0 / 0.7481 SEED-MEAN — reported under both protocols, mixed-protocol caveat explicit). The win arrives when gating and routing are added: 0.7774 — and it arrives via the confidence gate's fallback, not via pure routing (§5, §7).
4. **Match control budgets; endpoints are not best-seens.** The blind gardener's 0.855 at 10 moves became 0.748 (max−min 0.010, SD ≈ 0.0058) at the pre-registered 50-move budget. Stochastic-search baselines need budget-matched, multi-seed distributions (§5).
5. **Defend the evaluator, not just the model.** Sanity gates (5/5), shadow no-ops (5%), drift re-checks (1k states / 10 moves), and split-specific reporting exist because silent epistemic failure — not cell quality — is the dominant risk in agentic wiring loops (§5).
6. **Run the constant-baseline check before believing any judge agreement.** jev agreed with a student at 88/96 while labeling 93/96 (`blind_split`) and 96/96 (`blind_uniform`) cells needs-fix — a near-constant. One line of code separates a real judge from a threshold; the agreement CI is meaningless until the guard is checked (§6).
7. **Measure the routing surface; don't route on the documentation.** Cell signatures predict composition success (0.5955 bits, permutation null ≤ 0.0007 bits, p = 0.001) at ~5× the rate of the authored taxonomy (0.1212 bits, also vs its null, p = 0.001); only 15 of 64 regimes exist; the all-wrong basin (21.9%) is class-blind. The latent map is empirical (§7).
8. **Pre-register, commit-first, disclose deviations in the permanent record — including weak bars.** The degeneracy guard and the blind-control ruling bit precisely because they were frozen before the data. PX1b's post-hoc commit is disclosed and stands as the discipline's boundary case; the live-arm bar's >0.5 direction threshold is disclosed as weaker than we would pre-register today (§3, §5).

## 9 Limitations

**One microcosm.** Every state-space claim lives on 3×3 tic-tac-toe. The threat structure (WIN/BLOCK/NON_LOCAL) is real but small; nothing here shows the veto primitive matters at 19×19, in dialogue, or in code repair.

**The exact solver is a luxury — and here is the concrete scaling path we intend to test.** Most interesting domains have no enumeration. Three proxy paths, named so they can be falsified: (i) *cheap verifier ensembles* — where outputs are checkable (code against test suites, formal claims against checkers), a battery of independent verifiers gives a measured proxy ceiling in the spirit of §4's ladder; (ii) *self-play label farms* — labels generated and audited by the system itself with receipt trails, trading the solver's exactness for auditability; (iii) the *ActiveLedger doctrine* — ledger-first bookkeeping in which every claim carries its receipt, so proxy labels can be re-adjudicated wholesale when a stronger verifier arrives instead of silently rotting. None of these is validated at scale; we claim them as a plan, not a result.

**Judge constancy is shown on two synthetic fields only.** The 93/96 and 96/96 constancy is one judge (jev-1.13.0), one prompt format, two 96-cell synthetic fields. It establishes that the failure mode *exists and is detectable cheaply*, not that it is common. Replication plan: run the same constant-baseline check and distillation pipeline on real benchmark judges next (§6, §10).

**PX3's terrain is not the tic-tac-toe terrain.** The paper spans two microcosms (state-value enumeration; judged scene fields); the cross-field agreement result should not be read as a tic-tac-toe result.

**Router headroom: 26.7% claimed, the rest measured.** The +12.6 points is a measured upper bound over fixed cells. One pre-registered gated router claims 26.7% of it in a single deterministic run — no router-seed distribution yet, so the margin over d6, while above both protocols' d6 values, is one run deep. Pure routing KILLed its gate; the class-blind 21.9% basin bounds what routing alone can do.

**PX2's live arm is pending.** The GLM-5.3 gardener had fired six times — four fail-loud aborts (fires 1–4), a flag-gate abort at move 21 (fire 5), and fire 5b relaunched armed and in flight — with no final adjudication at revision time, so no live-agent number appears in this paper [CITE-MISSING: final adjudication, `results/px2_patchwork/`; lands as a numbered §5 addendum with its own timestamp].

**Exploratory status of PX5.** The signature map is pre-frozen EXPLORATORY_MAP: analyses were fixed before scoring, and the permutation nulls (§7) rule out estimator noise — but there is still no held-out confirmatory test of the *orthogonality* claim. PX6 confirmatory tests *routability*, which is adjacent but not identical. The per-state artifact persisted by PX5b makes a pre-registered held-out test runnable (§10).

**Minor protocol caveats.** The composition-vs-d6 comparison is reported under both protocols with the mixed-protocol caveat explicit (§5); PX1b's pre-registration was committed post-hoc (disclosed, §4), and the §5/§7 BLOCK diagnostic post-dates PX1b's result (disclosed, §4); set-recall is booked alongside top1 in all results files but this paper quotes top1 only.

## 10 Future work

**Router seeds and the NON_LOCAL wall.** Arm (b)'s 0.7774 needs a seed distribution before its margin is more than one deterministic run; pre-register router seeds 1/2 alongside the d6 ladder. The content result points at the real target: NON_LOCAL (0.522 with gating) is the wall, matching the class-blind basin — a router that dents the 21.9% is the experiment that matters, adjudicated partly on that dent.

**A held-out confirmatory test of the orthogonality claim.** PX5b's persisted per-state artifact (`per_state.npz`) makes this runnable: pre-register a split (e.g., by board symmetry class or position depth), estimate signature→success vs taxonomy→success on one half, and test the orthogonality asymmetry on the held-out half.

**Cross-domain replication.** Re-run the pipeline on a second exactly-enumerable domain (e.g. a bounded Connect-Four sublattice) and on verifier-backed real tasks (code repair against test suites). The three primitives — specialists, vetoes, measured routing surface — are only as general as their second instance.

**Judge calibration on real benchmarks, then distillation.** Re-run PX3's constant-baseline check and distillation pipeline on real benchmark judges after a calibration pass; the check stays in the harness permanently.

**PX4-style representation probes.** The queued information-through-ASCII probe (PX4 draft: raw grid vs canonicalized glyph alphabet vs shuffled-alphabet floor, same predictor budget) is the natural next instrument for the *representation* half of the story: how much routing-relevant signal survives compression of the cell inputs. The signature map gives it a target: a representation is good if the *signature structure* it induces stays interpretable and predictive, not merely if end-to-end top1 holds. (Reflex compile-back, listed here in v0, is demoted: PX6's coverage probe shows the pinch reflex already owns 88.9% of the train all-correct partition, so compile-back would add little on this terrain.)
