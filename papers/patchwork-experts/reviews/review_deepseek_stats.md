VERDICT: major revision

TOP CRITIQUES:

1. **§5, "Full-roster composition" — the headline comparison is not apples-to-apples and the paper admits it in a parenthetical.** 0.7437 (single deterministic combiner value) vs 0.7481 (3-seed mean) is presented as "at parity, slightly below (−0.4 points)." You cannot subtract a point estimate from a mean and call the sign of the difference a finding. Either report the combiner across the same 3 seeds with a spread, or report d6 at seed 0 only, or drop the "−0.4 points" framing entirely. As written, the "honest miss" is itself a dishonest comparison. This is the paper's central negative result and it is statistically under-specified.

2. **§6, PX3 — the degeneracy guard is applied to the wrong object, and the CI is decorative.** The guard fires because `blind_uniform` is 96/96 needs-fix. Fine. But then the paper reports a bootstrap CI [0.854, 0.969] on the 0.9167 agreement *anyway*, as if the CI were meaningful. A bootstrap CI on agreement against a constant label field is a CI on the majority-class rate of the student — it has no inferential content about distillation. Either suppress the CI when the guard fires, or explicitly relabel it as "CI on a degenerate quantity, shown only to demonstrate the guard's necessity." Also: 0.9167 = 88/96. State the raw counts, not just the rate, everywhere a rate is near a threshold (0.85 WIN zone). The reader cannot verify the boundary case from the decimal.

3. **§7, "The asymmetry" — mutual information is reported without estimator, bias correction, or uncertainty.** 0.5955 vs 0.1212 bits on 36,073 states with a 15-valued (or 64-valued, unclear) signature variable and a 3-valued threat class. MI estimators are notoriously biased upward with many bins; the signature variable has up to 64 categories, the threat class has 3. The "~5×" claim is the paper's central methodological point and it rests on two point estimates with no CIs, no bias correction (e.g., Miller-Madow, or a permutation null), and no statement of which variable's cardinality is being compared. A permutation test — shuffle Y, recompute MI, report the null distribution — is one line and would settle whether 0.1212 is distinguishable from zero. As written, the orthogonality claim is unfalsifiable from the text.

4. **§4, PX1b — the pre-registration deviation is disclosed but its consequences are not.** The classes and branch table were frozen in a docstring *before* the run but committed *after* with results. The paper treats this as a boundary case and moves on. But PX1b is load-bearing: the BLOCK split (0.022) is the empirical foundation for the entire veto-primitive argument, and the BLOCK split is reported in §5 and §7 as a running diagnostic. If the class definitions were frozen pre-run, fine — but the reader has no way to verify that from the text, and the paper does not say whether the *ordering prediction* (WIN > BLOCK > NON_LOCAL) was also frozen pre-run or constructed post-hoc to be falsified. The "honest ordering is WIN > NON_LOCAL > BLOCK" sentence reads as a post-hoc reframe. State explicitly which parts of PX1b were frozen pre-run and which were not, and whether the BLOCK-split diagnostic in §5/§7 was pre-registered or added after PX1b's result was known.

5. **§5, "The blind-control correction" — the correction is correct in spirit but the adjudication bar is under-specified and the live arm is cited as pending in a way that pre-commits the conclusion.** The bar is "beat the ~0.75 blind endpoint *and* pass a receipt-direction audit (>0.5 of moves whose predicted effect direction matches the measured direction)." Two problems. (a) ">0.5" is chance-level for a binary direction; a live agent that beats chance on direction is not evidence of anything. State the pre-registered threshold, not a post-hoc rationalization of what "pass" means. (b) The paper repeatedly asserts the live arm is pending and cites no numbers — good — but §5's closing sentence ("the veto specialist owns the defense split that voting cannot") is a conclusion drawn from the *blind* roster's receipts, not the live arm. That is fine, but it is presented in the same paragraph as the live-arm adjudication bar, which invites the reader to conflate them. Separate the blind-roster finding from the live-arm bar.

CONSISTENCY:

- **§4 vs §5 vs §7: the BLOCK-split numbers do not reconcile.** §4 reports BLOCK linear 0.022 ± 0.003, d3 0.24, d6 0.57, deep 0.997. §5 says the blind roster's BLOCK-split score "confirmed §4's prediction" but gives no number. §7 says the all-6-wrong basin is 21.9% and spreads WIN 2,812 / BLOCK 2,392 / NON_LOCAL 2,691. If BLOCK is 16.4% of the split (5,916 states) and 2,392 of those are all-6-wrong, that is 40.4% of BLOCK states where every tree is wrong — but §4 says d6 alone gets 0.57 on BLOCK, i.e., 57% correct. These are not necessarily contradictory (all-6-wrong is a stricter condition than d6-wrong), but the paper never reconciles them, and a reader will try. Add a sentence.

- **§3 vs §4: "180,361 reachable our-turn positions" vs PX1b class counts.** 109,392 + 29,632 + 41,337 = 180,361. Good. But the percentages given (60.6%, 16.4%, 22.9%) sum to 99.9%, not 100%. Rounding, presumably, but state it.

- **§5 vs §7: the composition score appears as 0.7437 in §5 and 0.7437 in §7, but §7's oracle is 0.8698 and §5's headroom is "+12.6 points."** 0.8698 − 0.7437 = 0.1261, i.e., +12.6 points. Consistent. But §5 says "the any-cell-correct ceiling on the same split is 0.8698" and §7 says "an oracle that picks any cell that is correct (including pinch) reaches 0.8698." Same number, same definition — good, but the paper should state once whether "any-cell-correct" includes the pinch cell's fired-correct states or not, because §7's parenthetical "(including pinch)" suggests §5's "any-cell-correct" might not. Clarify.

- **§6: "93–96 of 96 cells labeled needs-fix" in the abstract vs "93/96 on blind_split, 96/96 on blind_uniform" in §6.** The abstract's range is fine but the reader cannot tell which field is which. Minor, but the abstract is the only place a casual reader sees this number.

- **§1 vs §5: "a lucky 0.855 endpoint re-adjudicated to a budget-matched 0.748 ± 0.010."** §5 gives 0.745 / 0.745 / 0.755, mean 0.748, spread 0.010. Consistent. But "spread 0.010" is ambiguous — is that max−min (0.010) or SD? 0.745/0.745/0.755 has max−min = 0.010 and SD ≈ 0.0058. State which.

- **§3: "Three seeds (0/1/2) are run for variance" vs §5: "seeds 1/2/3" for the blind runs.** The blind runs use seeds 1/2/3, not 0/1/2. This is probably intentional (seed 0 was the 10-move run) but the paper never says so. A reader tracking seeds will be confused.

- **§7: "Of 64 possible tree-signatures, only 15 occur."** 64 = 2^6, correct for 6 binary trees. With only 15 observed signature values, the MI is over 15 × 2 = 30 cells, not 64 × 2. State the effective cardinality.
