# Patchwork Experts: Veto Primitives and the Routing Surface Between Cells

*Working note v0 (pre-panel draft). Quilt GPU Lab, 2026-09-30. Every number in this paper is booked in `FACTS.md` or in the cited results files of this repository; anything else is marked `[CITE-MISSING]`. One live experimental arm was still in flight at writing time; we cite no numbers for it.*

## Abstract

Composed "patchwork" systems — many shallow specialist cells wired by simple combiners — are usually evaluated end-to-end, which hides the primitives that make composition work. We study three such primitives on a microcosm where the ground truth is exact: every reachable 3×3 tic-tac-toe position is enumerated and solved (180,361 states, digest-frozen), so every claim is falsifiable against the enumeration rather than a sample. Three findings. (1) Additive voting collapses on defense: a linear voter places its top move in the optimal set on 2.2% of blocked positions, while an unpruned tree recovers the exact function (0.9987) — composed systems need explicit VETO primitives (gates, pinchers), not better voters. (2) A static full-roster composition reaches 0.7437, at parity with the best single shallow tree (0.7481); the headroom sits *between* cells: a per-state cell-signature predicts composition success (0.5955 bits of mutual information) far better than a hand-authored threat taxonomy (0.1212 bits), and an oracle router reaches 0.8698 (+12.6 points). (3) An LLM-as-judge distillation target is nearly a constant function (93–96 of 96 cells labeled needs-fix), so the pre-registered degeneracy guard caps the result at INCONCLUSIVE; constant-baseline checks must precede any judge distillation. All experiments ran under commit-first pre-registration, with deviations disclosed.

## 1 Introduction

Modern composed systems — sparse mixture-of-experts, ensembles, routers over model zoos, LLM pipelines stitched from tool calls — are usually argued about in the single-model vocabulary: capacity, loss, benchmark score. We claim this vocabulary hides three primitives that composed systems actually need:

1. **Dedicated specialist cells** whose competence is *local* to identifiable regions of the input space, not uniform;
2. **VETO primitives** — gates and pinchers that can override or abstain — because some failure modes of voting are not fixable by more or better voters;
3. **A measured routing surface BETWEEN cells** — the per-state pattern of which cells succeed — which is empirically a different, and more useful, object than any taxonomy the system's authors write down.

Studying these primitives at LLM scale is expensive and noisy: labels are contested, ceilings are unknown, and every result is confounded. We therefore build the argument on a microcosm where the ground truth is exact. The terrain is 3×3 tic-tac-toe: all 180,361 reachable our-turn positions, each labeled with the *set* of optimal moves from a minimax solver. Nothing is sampled; nothing is estimated from a test set of human labels. Every performance number below is a top-1-in-optimal rate over a frozen 36,073-state test split, and every claim can be re-derived from the committed enumeration (§3). The microcosm is small — that is the point. Small means exact, and exact means falsifiable: ceilings are measured quantities rather than assumptions, degeneracy is detectable rather than suspected, and failure basins can be attributed to the structure of the function rather than to annotation noise.

The paper is organized as a chain of pre-registered experiments (PX0–PX5), each frozen before it ran and each leaving receipts in the repository. §3 describes the terrain and the discipline. §4 shows additive voting collapsing on a specific, named failure mode (the defensive override) and derives the veto-primitive design consequence. §5 builds the patchwork harness with an immune layer against epistemic failure, and reports a blind-control correction (a lucky 0.855 endpoint re-adjudicated to a budget-matched 0.748 ± 0.010) plus a live-fire case study in receipt culture; the live LLM-gardener arm is disclosed as pending. §6 shows a real LLM-as-judge target that is nearly a constant function, and why a pre-registered degeneracy guard converts a flattering 0.9167 into an honest INCONCLUSIVE. §7 measures the routing surface between cells and finds it nearly orthogonal to the authored taxonomy. §8 condenses the results into design principles; §9 lists limitations; §10 lists next steps.

What we do *not* claim: no scaling claims of any kind; no claim that the current patchwork beats a deep tree (it does not, and §5 says so plainly); no claim that the router headroom is capturable — it is measured, not yet earned.

## 2 Related work

**Mixture-of-experts and routing.** Sparse MoE conditions computation on learned routers over expert subnetworks [CITE-MISSING: Shazeer et al., sparsely-gated mixture-of-experts, ICLR 2017]; scaled variants established the modern switching/expert-choice family [CITE-MISSING: Switch Transformer, JMLR 2022; expert-choice routing paper]. Classic hierarchical mixtures of experts treated gating as conditional probability mixture [CITE-MISSING: Jacobs, Jordan, Nowlan, Hinton, Neural Computation 1991]. This literature optimizes routers *jointly* with experts by gradient flow. Our microcosm studies the opposite regime: cells fixed and cheap, combiners trivial (majority vote, tie to lowest index), and the routing surface measured *post hoc* as an empirical object — closer to ensemble/abstention analysis [CITE-MISSING: deep ensembles; Chow's rejection rule] than to MoE training. Cascade classifiers are the nearest relative of our pinch cell: a cheap specialist that fires on a decisive sub-population and abstains otherwise [CITE-MISSING: Viola–Jones cascade classifier, CVPR 2001].

**LLM-as-judge.** Using a strong model to grade weaker outputs is now standard practice [CITE-MISSING: MT-Bench / LLM-as-judge, NeurIPS 2023], with known biases (position, verbosity, self-preference) documented [CITE-MISSING: judge-bias literature]. PX3 (§6) contributes a blunt addition to that list: a judge can be *approximately constant* on a whole evaluation field — agreeing with a distilled student at 0.9167 while carrying almost no per-cell structural signal. The check is one line; we argue it should be mandatory.

**Solved games as instrumentation.** Small solved games have long served as clean testbeds [CITE-MISSING: tic-tac-toe game-theoretic value, standard reference; endgame databases in checkers, Schaeffer et al., Science 2007; self-play line, AlphaZero, Science 2018]. We use the enumeration not to study play strength but as a *labeling instrument*: labels are optimal-move sets, so model quality is a membership test, not a preference judgment.

**Pre-registration culture.** Commit-first pre-registration, frozen branch tables, and deviation disclosure come from clinical trials and the replication movement [CITE-MISSING: CONSORT statement; Nosek et al. on preregistration, PNAS 2018; registered reports], and exist in ML mainly as checklists [CITE-MISSING: NeurIPS reproducibility checklist]. Here the discipline is load-bearing method: the PX3 degeneracy guard (§6) and the PX2 blind-control ruling (§5) both bite *because* they were frozen before the data existed. ## 3 An exact ground truth

**Terrain.** The donor repository (SuperInstance/pie-minimax, read-only clone at `main`) contains an exact minimax solver. We enumerate all 180,361 reachable our-turn positions and store, per state, the optimal-move set. Provenance is a FNV-1a-64 digest over the (state + optimal-set) stream: `0x75f652bc1d8464b8`. Representation for all learners is 9 floats per board (row-major), identical to the donor's own linear expert — no feature engineering advantage is traded in either direction.

**Metric.** Everywhere in this paper, model quality is

$$\mathrm{top1}(f) \;=\; \Pr_{s \in \text{test}}\big[\,\arg\max_c f(s)_c \in \mathcal{O}(s)\,\big],$$

where $\mathcal{O}(s)$ is the optimal-move set. Set-recall (fraction of $\mathcal{O}(s)$ recovered by the model's ranked moves) is reported alongside top1 in every booked results file, per the house rule that the pair always travels together. Chance-level top1 depends on $|\mathcal{O}(s)|$; all headline comparisons in this paper are model-vs-model on the same split, never model-vs-chance.

**Split.** PX1's seed-0 80/20 split: 144,288 train / 36,073 test. Three seeds (0/1/2) are run for variance; the split frozen at seed 0 is the one all later experiments (PX2, PX5) inherit. Trees are scikit-learn `DecisionTreeRegressor`, `min_samples_leaf=5`, `random_state=seed`, trained on the train split only.

**Why exactness matters.** Four reasons, each earning its keep later. (i) *Ceilings are measurements*: the deep-tree ceiling in §4 is a number, not a hope. (ii) *Controls are exact*: PX3's noise and oracle controls reproduce bit-exactly, so any drift found is the instrument's, not the terrain's. (iii) *Degeneracy is detectable*: a test field whose labels are single-class is provably useless for agreement claims, and we can assert that before believing a 0.9167. (iv) *Failure basins are attributable*: when 21.9% of the test split defeats every cell (§7), that is a fact about the function's structure, not about annotators.

**Pre-registration protocol.** Every PX experiment freezes its question, branch table, and controls before data collection. PX1/PX1b freeze in the runner module docstring; PX2 freezes as a standalone proposal file; PX3/PX5 freeze commit-first (the commit hash *is* the freeze: PX5 at `ecc8288`; PX3's chain `0b5b88d` → `ee99135` → `62f256a` → `f8c82e3`, pre-reg → instrument → receipts → result). One deviation is disclosed and stays disclosed: PX1b's pre-registration was frozen in the docstring *before* the run but committed *after*, alongside results — violating commit-first. We flag it in §4 where PX1b is used, and treat it as the boundary case that motivated the strict commit-first discipline used thereafter. House rules: outcomes may not switch branches post hoc; every deviation gets a dated amendment in the ledger (`RESULTS.md`); results book only to the pre-named results files.

## 4 The collapse of additive voters

**PX1 — the ceiling ladder.** On the frozen split, an unpruned tree reaches **0.9987 ± 0.0002** top1-in-optimal: the ceiling of this representation is, for practical purposes, the exact function. The donor's own linear expert — an additive voter over cell values — retrained on the same split reaches **0.1978 ± 0.005**, i.e. 19.8% of ceiling. The depth ladder interpolates: depth-3 **0.4811**, depth-4 **0.6024**, depth-6 **0.7481**. Two structural facts follow. First, the task is nearly learnable from 9 floats by a piecewise-constant function, so every gap below is a *representation/structure* gap, not a data gap. Second, most of the achievable win is cheap (d3 alone is 2.4× linear), which is what makes the microcosm a fair testbed for *shallow* cells.

**PX1's surprise, carried forward.** The pre-registered hypothesis was that additive voting fails where threats must be *counted* (composed states with multiple immediate wins). The data flipped the sign: linear voters do *better* on composed states (≥2 immediate wins: **0.2640**) than on simple ones (0–1 immediate wins: **0.1895**). Where several wins exist, each win lights up its own local terms and any of them tops the argmax — additive voting is *fine* on offense. Whatever kills the linear voter is not threat-counting.

**PX1b — isolating the failure mode.** PX1b partitions the enumeration by threat locality, classes frozen before the run, digest `0xa0b08dfac4d13c57`: **WIN** — an immediate win exists and all optimal moves are wins (109,392 states, 60.6%); **BLOCK** — no win exists, the opponent holds a two-in-line threat, and all optimal moves are blocking cells (29,632 states, 16.4%); **NON_LOCAL** — everything else (41,337 states, 22.9%). Per-class top1 on the test split (3 seeds):

| class | linear | d3 | d6 | deep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WIN (60.6%) | 0.25 | 0.55 | 0.84 | 0.9998 |
| BLOCK (16.4%) | **0.022 ± 0.003** | 0.24 | 0.57 | 0.997 |
| NON_LOCAL (22.9%) | 0.17 | 0.49 | 0.64 | 0.996 |

The ordered degradation the pre-registration predicted (WIN > BLOCK > NON_LOCAL) did not hold; the honest ordering is WIN > NON_LOCAL > BLOCK. Additive voting does not die on non-local play — it dies on **defense**. When the opponent holds a threat, the correct move is locally unremarkable, while the voter's own-line terms all light up in favor of continuing to build. A linear voter tops-1 the blocking cell on **2.2%** of BLOCK states; even a depth-3 tree only reaches 0.24 there. The deep tree does not (0.997): the defensive override is *representable* in 9 floats — it is just not *additive* and not *shallow*.

**Design consequence.** Voters score; they cannot veto. When the dominant failure mode is "the collective keeps saying yes to its own plan," the fix is not more voters or better-calibrated voters — it is a component with the *right and the ability to override*: a gate that vetoes illegal/degenerate outputs, a pincher that fires a known answer on a decisive sub-population and abstains otherwise, an immune layer that can refuse to act. PX2 was designed around this consequence, including a sharpened sub-test: every patchwork evaluation must report the BLOCK split specifically, because voting-only stacks bleed exactly there. *(Disclosure: PX1b's pre-registration was committed post-hoc with results — see §3. The classes and branch table were frozen in the docstring before the run; the deviation is in the ledger.)*

## 5 Patchwork composition and the immune layer

**Cells.** PX2's launch roster: depth-$k$ trees ($k \le 6$) trained on the train split only; a **PinchCell** (play center if empty, else block an opponent two-in-line; abstain otherwise — a hand-coded veto specialist built directly from §4); a **FormatGate** (structural check that a cell emitted 9 finite floats); a **MajorityVote** combiner (argmax of votes, ties to lowest index). Local small models and an embedding-based nearest-neighbor gate were wired but flag-gated off; nothing below depends on them.

**Smoke gate.** Before any gardener ran, the harness had to show that plain composition beats its own inputs: majority-vote(d3, d4) with the format gate scored **0.595** vs d3-alone **0.435** on the frozen 200-state quick-eval (+0.160). Composition passes its first honesty bar: wiring shallow cells is not a no-op.

**Full-roster composition — and an honest miss.** The complete static roster (trees d1–d6 plus pinch, MajorityVote) scores **0.7437** top1 on the full 36,073-state test split (measured inside PX5's instrument). The pre-registered cheap-baseline control says the patchwork must beat a depth-6 tree fit on identical train states; d6 is **0.7481**. The static composition is *at parity, slightly below* the best single shallow tree (−0.4 points). We report the miss as the finding it is: majority vote over a broad roster does not automatically beat the best member, and the justification for the patchwork program cannot rest on the static combiner. Where the justification *might* rest is measured in §7: the any-cell-correct ceiling on the same split is **0.8698**, i.e. +12.6 points of headroom that a routing layer between cells could in principle claim. (Caveat for the stats reviewer: the 0.7481 is a 3-seed mean per PX1 protocol; the 0.7437 is the deterministic combiner's single value as booked — the comparison is same-split but the seed protocols differ. Both numbers are as-booked in the results files.)

**The blind-control correction.** PX2's controls include a *blind gardener*: the same move-receipt protocol as the live agent, but moves drawn by seeded RNG from legal wiring moves. Its purpose is to price the harness's random-walk baseline so a live agent must beat it *and* pass a receipt audit. The first blind run (seed 0, 10 moves) ended at **0.855** — suspiciously high, and briefly celebrated. The correction is the part that matters: 10 moves is not the pre-registered budget, and the blind walk has **no ratchet** — the endpoint is where the walk stopped, not the best state seen along the way. Budget-matched serial 50-move runs (seeds 1/2/3) scored **0.745 / 0.745 / 0.755** — mean **0.748**, spread **0.010**. The pre-registered adjudication bar for the live arm (booked in the PX2 ledger entry) is therefore: beat the ~0.75 blind endpoint *and* pass a receipt-direction audit (>0.5 of moves whose predicted effect direction matches the measured direction). Two lessons generalize. First, single-endpoint anecdotes from stochastic search are noise; match the budget and run seeds before believing any delta. Second, the blind roster's discovered wiring included a pinch cell paired with d6, and its BLOCK-split score (booked in the PX2 receipts, `results/px2_patchwork/`, summarized in the ledger) confirmed §4's prediction in the running system: the veto specialist owns the defense split that voting cannot.

**The immune layer.** PX0 — an ideation round with three independent models — converged on the same diagnosis: the dominant risk in a gardener loop is not bad cells but *gardener epistemics* (format-pass/semantically-broken outputs, silent drift, post-hoc rationalization). Their proposed controls composed into PX2's immune layer, which ran on every session: (i) a cataloged sanity cell must pass a 5/5 gate against known ground-truth positions before any numbers are collected; (ii) 5% of gardener moves are silently replaced by no-ops, logged sidecar-only — if the gardener's receipt quality is indistinguishable between accepted and rejected moves, it is rationalizing, and all WIN/PARTIAL rulings are void; (iii) a fixed 1,000-state drift check re-scores every 10 moves; (iv) the BLOCK split is reported on every eval. None of these are exotic; all of them exist because the failure mode is the *evaluator*, not the model.

**The live-fire receipt chain.** The live arm — a GLM-5.3 gardener wiring cells via a coding-plan endpoint — is a case study in fail-loud infrastructure. Fire 1: the model's thinking mode consumed the 400-token output budget and returned empty content, cascading into abstentions (aborted, fail-loud). Fire 2 exposed the subtle one: `thinking: disabled` is honored on easy moves, but hard wiring moves trigger *adaptive* thinking that ignores the flag — a 17k-character reasoning trace, `finish_reason=length` at 4096. Fire 3 was killed for the fix (16,384 cap). Fire 4 hit a 120-second read timeout; fixed with a 240-second timeout, transport failures converted to *receipted abstains*, and a single clean same-prompt retry with no outcome information visible to the agent. Fire 5 was in flight at dossier time. Every abort left a receipt; no number from the live arm enters this paper. **[CITE-MISSING: final adjudication — the live GLM gardener's endpoint score and receipt-direction audit, to be booked in `results/px2_patchwork/` and cited only from there]**. Operational footnote from the ledger: same-second parallel launches collided on timestamped receipt filenames (three seeds, one file); serial launches are now the rule.

## 6 When the judge is a constant

**Setup.** PX3 tests whether a real LLM-as-judge can be *distilled*: a local student replacing the judge's 288 API calls per field with one forward pass. The judge is a hosted model (jev-1.13.0 via a typesafe `/v1/systemone` endpoint) answering correct/needs-fix per cell of an 8×12 synthetic field. Two blind fields (`blind_split`, `blind_uniform`), 96 judged cells each, rebuilt at three budgets: 576 calls total, 0 errors, receipts digest `0x29ebeffdce727a44`. The student embeds state strings with a local embedding model (768-d) and fits an L2-regularized logistic head in numpy — fully deterministic.

**The reproduction control earns its keep.** The house rule is that reproducing a stored result is a control, not a formality. Noise and oracle control MAEs reproduce bit-exactly; the uniform-field judge deltas reproduce exactly (+0.0000, ×3); but the split-field judge deltas drift in magnitude (direction holds), and a stored free-statistic correlation (−0.184) is irreproducible (fresh: −0.156) on an offline-*deterministic* quantity — that stored line predates the final fields module. Verdict: harness identical; the judge itself drifted across runs; the stored analysis was stale. Within-run, duplicate-triple label consistency is 1.0, so the drift is cross-run model-side, not within-run noise.

**Evals, and the guard that matters.** Within-field agreement on `blind_split` is 0.9583 (per-class F1 0.50 / 0.98 — with only 3 correct-class states, the F1 pair is exactly as lopsided as it should be); within-UNIFORM is 1.0 (trivial). The real claim is cross-field: trained on all `blind_split` cells, tested on all `blind_uniform` cells, the student agrees at **0.9167**, bootstrap CI **[0.854, 0.969]** (1,000 resamples, seed 0) — comfortably inside the pre-registered WIN zone (≥ 0.85). The pre-registration also froze a **degeneracy guard**: if the test field's labels are single-class, agreement cannot distinguish a student from a constant, and the ruling is capped at INCONCLUSIVE. The guard fires: `blind_uniform` is **96/96 needs-fix**. The flattering 0.9167 is capped to **INCONCLUSIVE**, as pre-committed.

**Mechanism discovery.** Why does a "judge ≈ noise" stored result coexist with 0.9167 agreement? Because the judge is *approximately a constant function on both blind fields*: **93/96** cells labeled needs-fix on `blind_split`, **96/96** on `blind_uniform`. A nearly-constant label function agrees with any student that predicts the majority label almost everywhere — and carries no structural signal to distill. The stored "judge ≈ noise" was never about judgment quality in the human sense; it is what a threshold-biased near-constant looks like in a noise-delta harness. The three `correct` outliers on the split field are the only structure present, and the student found two of them.

**Consequences.** (i) Any LLM-as-judge distillation — arguably any judge *use* — must run a constant-baseline check before any agreement number is interpreted. The check is one line: label distribution of the field, and student-vs-majority-label agreement. (ii) Judges need calibration audits per prompt-format, not per benchmark: this judge's threshold/bias on scene-format prompts makes it a selector that selects nothing. (iii) The pre-registered guard did the exact job it was frozen for: it converted a publishable-looking WIN into an honest INCONCLUSIVE *before* anyone had to decide under incentive load.

## 7 The signature map

PX5 asks the question the single-model view cannot even pose: *what does the space between cells look like?* The instrument (frozen commit-first at `ecc8288`, classed EXPLORATORY_MAP — exploratory, no confirmatory branch): for every test state, record the 6-bit **signature** of which trees d1–d6 place their top-1 in the optimal set, crossed with the pinch cell's axis (fired-correct / fired-wrong / abstain), the PX1b threat class, and whether the full composition (§5) is correct. Terrain digest asserted; full 36,073-state test split; no training involved — this is pure measurement of an existing system.

**The census.** Of 64 possible tree-signatures, only **15** occur across 36,073 states. The cells' success patterns are heavily structured: the six trees do not behave as interchangeable noisy voters; they carve the state space into a few large, repeatable regimes.

**The asymmetry.** Mutual information, $I(S;Y) = \sum_{s,y} p(s,y)\log_2 \frac{p(s,y)}{p(s)p(y)}$, in bits:

- $I(\text{signature} \to \text{composition-correct}) = \mathbf{0.5955}$
- $I(\text{signature} \to \text{threat class}) = \mathbf{0.1212}$

Knowing *which cells succeed here* strongly predicts whether the composition gets the state — and barely predicts the hand-authored WIN/BLOCK/NON_LOCAL taxonomy. The routing surface is nearly **orthogonal to the author taxonomy**: it is a latent segmentation of the state space that was *discovered by the instrument*, not designed by the authors. This is the paper's central methodological point about composed systems: the map that matters for routing is not the taxonomy in the documentation; it is the empirical correlation structure of cell success, and it is cheap to measure.

**Unanimous partitions.** 9,470 states (26.3%) are all-6-correct — reflex-compile candidates: regions where every cell already agrees correctly and a verified fast path could replace the vote. 7,895 states (**21.9%**) are all-6-wrong — a class-blind failure basin, spread remarkably evenly across the authored classes (WIN 2,812 / BLOCK 2,392 / NON_LOCAL 2,691). No current class vocabulary explains the basin; whatever it is, the cells share a systematic bias that the threat taxonomy does not name. This is a concrete, falsifiable target: any new cell or training change should be adjudicated partly on whether it dents the 21.9%.

**Router headroom.** An oracle that picks any cell that is correct (including pinch) reaches **0.8698** vs the composition's 0.7437 — **+12.6 points**. We emphasize the epistemic status: this is *measured headroom*, an existence proof that routing between these cells could pay, not a claim that a trained router will capture it. The signature census suggests the capture problem is tractable (15 regimes, not 64), and the failure basin warns where it will stall.

**Pinch pre-emption.** The pinch cell fires on 72.5% of states (66.35% correct, 6.15% wrong) and abstains on 27.5%. A hand-coded two-rule veto specialist is *correct on the vast majority of the space where it dares speak* — and its 6.15% wrong-fire rate is exactly the kind of number a routing layer must learn to gate, not to ignore.

## 8 Design principles

1. **Measure the ceiling before interpreting any score.** Linear 0.1978 reads as "bad model" until the deep tree shows 0.9987 — then it reads as "additive representation, 19.8% of ceiling." Every fraction-of-ceiling claim in a composed system should be backed by a measured ceiling (§4).
2. **Additive voters cannot veto; give the system explicit VETO primitives.** The defensive override (BLOCK: linear 0.022, d3 0.24, deep 0.997) is invisible to aggregate scores and fatal in play. Gates and pinchers that can override or abstain are a different *kind* of component, not a better voter (§4).
3. **Composition must beat its parts — including the best single cheap member — and say so when it doesn't.** d3+d4+gate beats d3 (0.595 vs 0.435); the full static roster does not beat d6 (0.7437 vs 0.7481). Report the miss; locate the headroom honestly (§5).
4. **Match control budgets; endpoints are not best-seens.** The blind gardener's 0.855 at 10 moves became 0.748 ± 0.010 at the pre-registered 50-move budget. Stochastic-search baselines need budget-matched, multi-seed distributions (§5).
5. **Defend the evaluator, not just the model.** Sanity gates (5/5), shadow no-ops (5%), drift re-checks (1k states / 10 moves), and split-specific reporting exist because silent epistemic failure — not cell quality — is the dominant risk in agentic wiring loops (§5).
6. **Run the constant-baseline check before believing any judge agreement.** jev agreed with a student at 0.9167 while being 93–96/96 needs-fix — a near-constant. One line of code separates a real judge from a threshold (§6).
7. **Measure the routing surface; don't route on the documentation.** Cell signatures predict composition success (0.5955 bits) at ~5× the rate of the authored taxonomy (0.1212 bits); only 15 of 64 regimes exist; the all-wrong basin (21.9%) is class-blind. The latent map is empirical (§7).
8. **Pre-register, commit-first, and disclose deviations in the permanent record.** The degeneracy guard and the blind-control ruling bit precisely because they were frozen before the data. PX1b's post-hoc commit is disclosed and stands as the discipline's boundary case (§3, §4).

## 9 Limitations

**One microcosm.** Every state-space claim lives on 3×3 tic-tac-toe. The threat structure (WIN/BLOCK/NON_LOCAL) is real but small; nothing here shows the veto primitive matters at 19×19, in dialogue, or in code repair. **The exact solver is a luxury.** Most interesting domains have no enumeration; our discipline — ceilings as measurements, degeneracy as assertion — needs proxies (synthetic solvable sublattices, verifier-backed test suites) that we have not yet validated at scale. **Judge constancy is shown on two synthetic fields only.** The 93–96/96 constancy is one judge (jev-1.13.0), one prompt format, two 96-cell synthetic fields. It establishes that the failure mode *exists and is detectable cheaply*, not that it is common. **PX3's terrain is not the tic-tac-toe terrain.** The paper spans two microcosms (state-value enumeration; judged scene fields); the cross-field agreement result should not be read as a tic-tac-toe result. **Router headroom is measured, not claimed.** The +12.6 points is an oracle bound over fixed cells; no trained router has captured any of it yet, and the class-blind 21.9% basin bounds what routing alone can do. **PX2's live arm is pending.** The GLM-5.3 gardener had fired five times with four fail-loud aborts and a fix chain; no final adjudication existed at writing time, so no live-agent number appears in this paper [CITE-MISSING: final adjudication, `results/px2_patchwork/`]. **Exploratory status of PX5.** The signature map is pre-frozen EXPLORATORY_MAP: analyses were fixed before scoring, but there is no held-out confirmatory test of the orthogonality claim. **Minor protocol caveats.** The static-composition vs d6 comparison crosses a single deterministic value with a 3-seed mean on the same split (§5); PX1b's pre-registration was committed post-hoc (disclosed, §4); set-recall is booked alongside top1 in all results files but this paper quotes top1 only.

## 10 Future work

**The signature-router cell.** Train a router on the 6-bit signature (plus pinch axis) to select among cells per state, with the pre-registered bar: beat d6 (0.7481) on the full split *and* beat the static composition (0.7437), with the 21.9% all-wrong basin reported separately as "routing cannot help here (yet)."

**Cross-domain replication.** Re-run the pipeline on a second exactly-enumerable domain (e.g. a bounded Connect-Four sublattice) and on verifier-backed real tasks (code repair against test suites). The three primitives — specialists, vetoes, measured routing surface — are only as general as their second instance.

**Reflex compile-back to fleet memory.** The 9,470 all-correct states (26.3%) are candidates for compiling verified reflexes — fast paths that skip the vote where every cell already agrees, each compiled path carrying its own receipt and a drift re-check from the immune layer.

**Judge calibration, then distillation.** Re-run PX3 on a non-degenerate field after a calibration pass on the judge's threshold/bias; the constant-baseline check stays in the harness permanently.

**PX4-style representation probes.** The queued information-through-ASCII probe (PX4 draft: raw grid vs canonicalized glyph alphabet vs shuffled-alphabet floor, same predictor budget) is the natural next instrument for the *representation* half of the story: how much routing-relevant signal survives compression of the cell inputs. The signature map gives it a target: a representation is good if the *signature structure* it induces stays interpretable and predictive, not merely if end-to-end top1 holds.
